# Insights: 2026 Institutional Trends
*Synthesized from board and investment committee activity across 118 U.S. public pension plans (~$4.8 trillion AUM)*
*Generated: August 28, 2026*

---

## 1. Strong FY2026 Returns Mask a Deteriorating Forward Outlook

Fiscal year 2026 delivered robust absolute returns across plans reporting through June 30. **KPPA** (~$20B) posted 13.80% for the CERS pension fund versus a 13.33% benchmark ([source](?doc=5850)). **ERS-GA** (~$20B) reported approximately 16.5% for FY2026 ([source](?doc=5673)). **SERS Ohio** (~$18B) returned 18.86% net over one year versus 16.16% for the policy benchmark ([source](?doc=5219)). **NHRS** (~$9B) achieved a preliminary 11.83% net return for FY2026 ([source](?doc=5698)). **SMCERA** (~$5B, as of June 30, 2026 at $8.05B) reported a trailing twelve-month net return of 14.2% ([source](?doc=5752)). **TRS Texas** (~$200B) returned 14.4% over one year ending March 31, 2026, versus a 13.1% benchmark ([source](?doc=5236)).

Yet forward capital market assumptions are compressing. **KPPA**'s Wilshire 2026 assumptions show the current CERS strategic allocation's expected 10-year return at 6.27%, approximately 25 basis points below the 6.5% discount rate, with private equity's 10-year expected return reduced from 9.2% (2023) to 6.6% (2026) ([source](?doc=5850)). **LACERA** (~$75B) received updated Meketa 2026 capital market expectations showing broadly lower expected returns across most asset classes due to higher equity valuations and tighter credit spreads ([source](?doc=5499)). **STRS Ohio** (~$87B) forecasts a total fund return of 6.4% in its Fiscal 2027 Investment Plan, below the 7.0% actuarial target ([source](?doc=4926)). **ATRS** (~$20B) is considering lowering its assumed rate from 7.25% to 7.15% or 7.10%, with GRS noting that maintaining 7.25% may lead to future actuarial reasonableness concerns; the NASRA average across 132 public systems is 6.91% ([source](?doc=5812)).

*Sources: [KPPA — Board Pack — August 26, 2026](?doc=5850), [ERS-GA — Agenda — August 20, 2026](?doc=5673), [SERS Ohio — Board Pack — July 16, 2026](?doc=5219), [NHRS — Minutes — August 01, 2026](?doc=5698), [SMCERA — Board Pack — August 25, 2026](?doc=5752), [TRS Texas — Board Pack — July 01, 2026](?doc=5236), [LACERA — Agenda — August 12, 2026](?doc=5499), [STRS Ohio — Board Pack — June 25, 2026](?doc=4926), [ATRS — Board Pack — August 25, 2026](?doc=5812)*

**Bottom line:** Plans are banking strong trailing returns while their own consultants signal that repeating those returns over the next decade is unlikely — creating a narrow window for boards to lock in assumption reductions and accelerate liability-hedging shifts before the next drawdown.

## 2. Infrastructure and Digital Infrastructure Emerge as Dominant Commitment Themes

Infrastructure commitments are appearing across plans of all sizes with notable frequency. **MainePERS** (~$18B, as of June 30, 2026 at ~$23.0B) committed up to $300 million to **Brookfield Super-Core Infrastructure Partners** ([source](?doc=5581)). **TRS-OK** (~$19B) approved $150 million to **SDC Digital Infrastructure Opportunity Fund V** ([source](?doc=5813)). **SJCERA** (~$2B) committed $100 million to **I Squared Global Infrastructure Fund IV** ([source](?doc=4976)). **OPPRS** (~$2B) is evaluating open-end infrastructure search finalists ([source](?doc=5721)). **JPFPF** (~$2B) previewed a potential commitment to **Blue Owl Digital Infrastructure Fund IV** but ultimately declined, citing existing data-center exposure through Fund III ([source](?doc=5868)). **TRSL** (~$25B) is reviewing **ISQ Global Infrastructure Fund IV** ([source](?doc=5810)).

The data-center sub-theme is particularly pronounced. **LAFPP** (~$29B) received an educational presentation on digital infrastructure and data centers from **Townsend** and **Principal Real Estate Investors** ([source](?doc=5674)). **JPFPF**'s executive director flagged political/regulatory risk, electricity and water requirements, and community concerns around data-center investments ([source](?doc=5868)). **TRS Texas** highlighted data center power demand as a major investment theme, noting hyperscaler capital investment exceeding $700 billion by 2026 ([source](?doc=5236)).

*Sources: [MainePERS — Agenda — August 13, 2026](?doc=5581), [TRS-OK — Agenda — August 26, 2026](?doc=5813), [SJCERA — Agenda — July 10, 2026](?doc=4976), [OPPRS — Agenda — August 19, 2026](?doc=5721), [JPFPF — Board Pack — August 21, 2026](?doc=5868), [TRSL — Agenda — August 31, 2026](?doc=5810), [LAFPP — Agenda — August 20, 2026](?doc=5674), [TRS Texas — Board Pack — July 01, 2026](?doc=5236)*

**Practical implication:** Infrastructure is no longer a niche allocation — it is becoming a core portfolio building block, with digital infrastructure as the fastest-growing sub-sector; plans that have not yet built pacing models for this space risk being late to an increasingly competitive fundraising cycle.

## 3. Aggressive Fee Discipline and Passivization Accelerate

Fee management has moved from aspiration to measurable execution. **KPPA** staff renegotiated public high-yield manager fees down 12%, saving approximately $500,000 annually, and negotiated 12 of 14 managers into the lowest fee quartile ([source](?doc=5850)). Investment fees at KPPA declined 4% year-over-year despite a 13% increase in total assets ([source](?doc=5739)). **MainePERS** reported its fee ratio declining from 0.74% (FY23) to 0.58% (FY26) ([source](?doc=5033)). **LACERS** (~$25B) is adopting NEPC's "Model 2" for non-U.S. equities, increasing passive management from 41.5% to 64.5% and reducing estimated manager fees by 12 basis points ([source](?doc=5066)). **LAFPP** noted **Brandes Investment Partners**' fee of 20 basis points ranks in the first percentile of the eVestment ACWI ex US Large Cap Value universe ([source](?doc=4852)). **LACERS** also highlighted **PGIM**'s fee of 33 basis points ranking in the 11th percentile ([source](?doc=5767)).

Simultaneously, active managers facing underperformance are being terminated. **LACERS** allowed the **Lazard Asset Management** contract to expire due to persistent underperformance ([source](?doc=5066)). **DPFP** (~$3B) terminated **Manulife** Global Equity and hired **Arrowstreet** for up to a 6% allocation ([source](?doc=5645)). **MainePERS** authorized full redemption of the **King Street Capital** strategy ([source](?doc=5581)).

*Sources: [KPPA — Board Pack — August 26, 2026](?doc=5850), [KPPA — Board Pack — August 19, 2026](?doc=5739), [MainePERS — Agenda — July 09, 2026](?doc=5033), [LACERS — Agenda — July 14, 2026](?doc=5066), [LAFPP — Agenda — July 01, 2026](?doc=4852), [LACERS — Agenda — August 25, 2026](?doc=5767), [DPFP — Agenda — August 07, 2026](?doc=5645), [MainePERS — Agenda — August 13, 2026](?doc=5581)*

**Bottom line:** The fee squeeze is structural, not cyclical — plans are simultaneously passivizing public markets, hardening fee benchmarks, and terminating underperformers, compressing the economics for active managers who cannot demonstrate persistent alpha.

## 4. Liability-Hedging Shifts and Fixed Income Build-Outs Gain Momentum

Several plans are deliberately rotating from return-seeking to liability-hedging assets. **SMCERA** approved "Policy X," reducing return-seeking assets by 10% and increasing liability-hedging assets by 10% ([source](?doc=5752)). **STRS Ohio** added $665 million to fixed income and removed $1 billion from domestic equity in May 2026 alone, with fixed income receiving over $6 billion in net allocations during fiscal 2026 ([source](?doc=4926)). **EPCP** (~$2B) approved a $14 million rebalance from US Equity to Fixed Income and Cash to restore IPS compliance ([source](?doc=5781)). **SERS Ohio** approved an FY2027 Annual Investment Plan with a Global Fixed Income target of 18% ([source](?doc=5219)).

This trend runs counter to the common assumption that strong equity returns would delay de-risking. Plans appear to be using the FY2026 equity rally as an opportunity to rebalance toward policy targets rather than letting equity overweights ride.

*Sources: [SMCERA — Board Pack — August 25, 2026](?doc=5752), [STRS Ohio — Board Pack — June 25, 2026](?doc=4926), [EPCP — Minutes — July 01, 2026](?doc=5781), [SERS Ohio — Board Pack — July 16, 2026](?doc=5219)*

**Practical implication:** The window for fixed income managers to capture new mandates is open now — plans are actively deploying into core and core-plus strategies while equity valuations provide favorable rebalancing conditions.

## 5. AI Governance and Investment Theme Adoption Accelerate Simultaneously

AI is appearing in pension board activity in two distinct channels: as an operational tool and as an investment theme. **TRS Texas** allocated $2.5 million for strategic AI technology investments in FY2027 and approved 24 new FTEs, 16 of which are for AI technology initiatives ([source](?doc=5235)). TRS Texas also formed an AI Taskforce identifying five long-term structural AI investment themes ([source](?doc=5236)). **OCERS** (~$25B) created a new AI Automation Developer position budgeted at $249,489 annually to support AI, LLM, Microsoft Copilot, and enterprise AI governance ([source](?doc=5821)). **FPPA** (~$6B) is implementing AI tools including Claude, Microsoft Visual Studio Code, and Adobe Firefly, and has established an AI Governance Committee ([source](?doc=5718)). **LACERA** presented estimated AI exposures across the Total Fund and OPEB Master Trust ([source](?doc=5499)). **VRS** (~$110B) reported its risk management team researched AI portfolio exposure quantification ([source](?doc=5580)). **DCRB** (~$13B) noted its Benefits Committee is exploring use of artificial intelligence in operations ([source](?doc=5359)).

*Sources: [TRS Texas — Board Pack — July 01, 2026](?doc=5235), [TRS Texas — Board Pack — July 01, 2026](?doc=5236), [OCERS — Agenda — August 31, 2026](?doc=5821), [FPPA — Board Pack — August 20, 2026](?doc=5718), [LACERA — Agenda — August 12, 2026](?doc=5499), [VRS — Board Pack — August 12, 2026](?doc=5580), [DCRB — Minutes — July 16, 2026](?doc=5359)*

**Practical implication:** AI is no longer a future-state discussion item — it is being budgeted, staffed, and governed today, creating both a new category of operational risk that boards must oversee and a thematic investment lens that is reshaping how plans evaluate portfolio exposures.

## 6. Geopolitical Risk Reshapes Market Assumptions and Portfolio Stress Testing

The Iran–Strait of Hormuz conflict is the dominant geopolitical factor cited across plans in 2026. **KPPA** noted the conflict caused significant market volatility starting late February 2026, with cargo travel halted and oil prices spiking; one-year inflation expectations jumped though 5- and 10-year expectations remained relatively stable ([source](?doc=5739)). **TRS Texas** reported commodities were the top-performing asset class in Q1 2026, with the Bloomberg Commodity Index returning +24.4%, driven by geopolitical tensions and rising oil prices ([source](?doc=5236)). **LACERA** cited ongoing Middle East tensions disrupting Strait of Hormuz transit as contributing to higher oil prices and inflation risks ([source](?doc=5499)). **VRS** modeled four economic scenarios through 2045 including Stagflation and Fiscal Dominance, with the latter projecting US debt exceeding 125% of GDP and inflation of 3-6% ([source](?doc=5580)). **EPCP** noted inflation decelerated to 3.5% YoY in June from 4.2% in May, influenced by a Strait of Hormuz ceasefire/peace deal ([source](?doc=5781)).

*Sources: [KPPA — Board Pack — August 19, 2026](?doc=5739), [TRS Texas — Board Pack — July 01, 2026](?doc=5236), [LACERA — Agenda — August 12, 2026](?doc=5499), [VRS — Board Pack — August 12, 2026](?doc=5580), [EPCP — Minutes — July 01, 2026](?doc=5781)*

**Bottom line:** The Iran conflict has served as a live stress test for pension portfolios, validating real assets and commodities allocations while exposing the inflation sensitivity of traditional fixed income — plans with meaningful real return allocations, like KPPA (Real Return: 16.49% vs. 7.25% benchmark for FY2026), materially outperformed in those sleeves ([source](?doc=5850)).

## 7. Co-Investment and Internal Management Capabilities Expand

Plans are investing in internal capacity to reduce fees and improve deal access. **OCERS** is adding a dedicated Investment Officer (Private Equity) to expand its co-investment program from 10% to 20% of the annual PE pacing plan, with co-investment check sizes expected to grow from $10 million to $20 million ([source](?doc=5821)). OCERS cited co-investments as generating +400 basis points potential return enhancement with no fee or carry versus fund investments ([source](?doc=5821)). **SIB-ND** (~$8B) reported internal portfolios now represent approximately $4.2 billion of RIO's $28.8 billion total program, with plans to expand active management capabilities ([source](?doc=4959)). **VRS** launched a prime brokerage margin lending program and is exploring self-directed lending and tri-party repo initiatives to expand funding channels ([source](?doc=5580)). **STRS Ohio** is increasing allocation to internal quantitative strategies as a priority ([source](?doc=4926)).

*Sources: [OCERS — Agenda — August 31, 2026](?doc=5821), [SIB-ND — Board Pack — July 10, 2026](?doc=4959), [VRS — Board Pack — August 12, 2026](?doc=5580), [STRS Ohio — Board Pack — June 25, 2026](?doc=4926)*

**Practical implication:** The build-versus-buy calculus is shifting — larger plans are internalizing capabilities and scaling co-investment programs, which will further compress fee revenue for external managers and increase competitive pressure on GPs to offer co-investment access as a standard LP benefit.
