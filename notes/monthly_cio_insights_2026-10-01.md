## 1. Fiscal-Year Performance Strength, with Pockets of Benchmark-Lagging

Public pension plans closing out FY2026 reported broadly positive net returns. **CalSTRS** ($340B, CA) posted a 13.9% net return for the fiscal year, pushing total fund assets past $400 billion as of June 30, 2026 (doc_id=7014). **VRS** ($110B, VA) returned 12.1% net against a 6.75% assumed rate, growing assets to approximately $136 billion (doc_id=7449). **TRS Texas** ($200B, TX) returned 8.5% in Q2 2026 versus a 7.5% Policy Benchmark, with a one-year return of 16.5% including 146 bps of excess return (doc_id=7027). **MN SBI** ($161B, MN) Combined Funds returned 15.6% for fiscal year 2026, outperforming its 14.6% composite benchmark (doc_id=7077). **IPERS** ($41B, IA) posted 12.88% net versus a 12.56% policy benchmark (doc_id=7046), while **SERS Ohio** ($18B, OH) returned 16.82% versus a 12.93% benchmark (doc_id=7429).

Several plans trailed benchmarks: **LACERA** ($75B, CA) returned 7.3% FYTD net, versus a 9.3% policy benchmark (doc_id=7038); **WSIB** ($200B, WA) returned 10.67% over one year versus an 18.01% passive benchmark (doc_id=7071); and **ATRS** ($20B, AR) returned 15.8% versus a 16.6% benchmark (doc_id=7065). This divergence continued into late October: **PSPRS** ($28.1B, AZ) returned 13.9% net over one year versus a 20.8% policy benchmark, with Global Private Equity returning 7.4% versus a 37.0% benchmark (doc_id=9638). By contrast, **ILSRS** ($25B, IL) reported a 12-month return of 10.9%, with Global Equity returning 6.5% against an 8.1% benchmark and Infrastructure returning 2.6% against a 5.0% benchmark (doc_id=11410), while **SCERS Seattle** ($4B, WA) returned 14.78% net versus a 14.49% benchmark on $5.3 billion in assets, with funded status of 80.5% (doc_id=11755). **SamCERA** ($5B, CA) posted a 14.37% money-weighted FY2026 return against a 5.1% assumed rate, improving funded ratio to 92.4% (doc_id=11515). **NCRS** ($120B, NC) TSERS returned 12.92% market value versus a 6.50% assumed rate, lifting funded ratio to 90.0% (doc_id=11589).

**Practical implication: Allocators should scrutinize benchmark construction plan-by-plan — strong headline fiscal-year returns coexist with material active/passive and private-equity benchmark shortfalls at plans like PSPRS and WSIB, warranting manager-level attribution before drawing fund-level conclusions.**

## 2. Private Markets Commitment Pace Remains Elevated Across Plan Sizes

Private markets deployment continued at scale throughout October. **NYSCRF** ($270B, NY) committed $300 million to Artemis Real Estate Partners Fund V and $115.9 million to a Texas student-housing acquisition through BlackRock (doc_id=7069), alongside July commitments exceeding $815 million, including $200 million to Northern Trust's Emerging Markets Index Fund and $250 million to Excelsior Beyond CIV L.P. (doc_id=7070). **VRS** committed $200 million to Audax PE VIII, $300 million to Castlelake Asset-Based Private Credit IV, and $400 million to JP Morgan Hybrid Growth (doc_id=7449). **MainePERS** ($18B, ME) approved $500 million to Ares Senior Direct Lending Core and $280 million to Meridiam North America Core Infrastructure Fund I (doc_id=7135). **ATRS** approved $245 million across five funds including Consonance Private Equity III and Digital Realty Flagship Fund-Americas (doc_id=7065).

Activity accelerated into the final week of the month. **NYSTRS** ($135B, NY) approved a wave of commitments spanning real estate, infrastructure debt, and private equity, including a $300 million renewal commitment with co-investment and two private equity commitments of $200 million and $150 million (doc_id=11274). **TRS-OK** ($19B, OK) moved to approve combined commitments of up to $1.13 billion to the Apollo Investment Grade Short Duration Fund and Blackstone Credit Rose Rock LP (doc_id=11276). **CCCERA** ($10B, CA) disclosed new commitments to Leonard Green Equity X, RCG Ventures Fund VIII ($50 million), and Hines Rialto Credit Partners ($100 million) (doc_id=11707). **LASERS** ($13B, LA) committed up to $100 million to the GoldenTree Distressed Fund (doc_id=11659), and **JPFPF** ($2B, FL) approved a $15 million commitment to Bell Value-Add Fund IX with Bell Partners despite Bell's acquisition by BGO (doc_id=11264), having listed the same re-up as an agenda action item the week prior (doc_id=9740). One commitment failed to close: **SamCERA**'s $15 million allocation to Silver Creek Manufactured Housing Communities failed on re-vote 2-6-0 after being misreported as approved (doc_id=11885).

**Practical implication: The breadth of commitment activity — from $15 million re-ups to $1.13 billion credit mandates — indicates private markets capital calls remain a primary allocation lever regardless of plan size, though governance diligence on vote reporting (as in SamCERA's reversed vote) deserves LP follow-up.**

## 3. Active International/Small-Cap Equity Mandates Under Pressure

A recurring pattern across the month was the unwinding of active international and small-cap equity mandates in favor of passive or replacement strategies. **SERS PA** ($35B, PA) approved a $2 billion reallocation from Xponance's active International Small Cap strategy to a passive U.S. Equity strategy (doc_id=7444). **DPFRS** ($3B, MI) terminated Allsprings International Small Cap, reallocating to Rhumbline Advisors (doc_id=7471). **DPERS** ($12B, DE) terminated Dodge & Cox International ($358 million) and redeemed from Baillie Gifford LTGG ($290 million) (doc_id=7148). **NYSCRF** terminated its $1.2 billion Mondrian Investment Partners international equity position (doc_id=7069). Later in the month, **ARMB** ($25B, AK) finalized an agreement to add Jennison as a manager in its international equity white-label fund, replacing Baillie Gifford (doc_id=11305), and **Metro Nashville ERS** ($2B, TN) terminated Champlain as mid-cap manager after extended underperformance, transitioning assets to the BNY Mid Cap Index (doc_id=11267).

**Practical implication: The repeated termination of active international and small-cap managers across SERS PA, DPFRS, DPERS, NYSCRF, ARMB and Metro Nashville ERS points to a broad institutional re-underwriting of active management value in these sleeves — allocators with similar mandates should expect continued fee and structure renegotiation pressure.**

## 4. Governance Restructuring and Leadership Transitions

Governance activity spanned compensation, board composition, and committee structure. **TRS Texas** established a Board Special Committee for a CIO search and increased CIO Jase Auby's salary cap to $911,000 plus a $60,000 one-time payment (doc_id=7023). **PERA Colorado** ($60B, CO) reviewed Aon/McLagan benchmarking, recommending CEO Andrew Roth's maximum incentive rise from 25% to 50% of salary (doc_id=7035). **LACERA** dissolved three asset-class committees and moved to a six-meeting annual cadence starting 2027 (doc_id=7038). **IPERS** appointed Martin Noven as CEO with a starting salary of $280,000 (doc_id=7047). Mid-month, **TRS-GA** (~$120B, GA) saw its Investment Committee unanimously endorse Stephanie Lang, founder/CEO of Armor Point Advisors, for appointment to the Board effective October 28, 2026, filling the vacancy left by Douglas R. Aldridge Jr.'s resignation (doc_id=8925).

By month-end, structural change intensified at the state level: **NCRS** ($120B, NC) reported that five bills enacted during North Carolina's 2026 legislative session reduced the TSERS Board of Trustees from 13 voting members to 7 plus 1 non-voting member and the LGERS board from 13 to 7 members, both effective July 1, 2027, while providing a one-time 2.5% supplement to TSERS, CJRS, and LRS retirees payable October 15, 2026 (doc_id=11585). **OPERS** ($97B, OH) approved its 2027 Investment Division Incentive Compensation Plan 8-0 and raised Member-Directed Plan administrative fees from 0.26% to up to 0.50% (doc_id=11275). **NYSTRS** adopted amendments simplifying its Public Equities provisions and a five-year de-risking glide path, both unanimous (doc_id=11274).

**Practical implication: The shift from committee-level governance tweaks earlier in the month to statutory board restructuring at NCRS by month-end signals that governance reform in October 2026 was not confined to single plans but reflects a broader legislative and compensation recalibration across multiple state systems.**
